### RL-2 — Interim decision authority for approval tasks

*Date:* 22 October 2026 · *Ruled by:* ct · *Governs:* PEG-005, PEG-036

Interim decision authority (ct-ruled 22 Oct 2026): a workspace owner or administrator who is not the approval task's owner approves it, requests changes or rejects it, against its exact current revision. Anyone who has owned the task since its previous decision, and its owner at that decision, is also refused. An approval task's owner is the person accountable for the work under review, never its approver; its requester may decide. A personal approval task cannot be decided until PEG-073's reviewed share places it in a project. A task whose only eligible decider owns or owned it stays pending, and the decision preview says why without naming anyone (personal share required, you own or owned this work, another administrator needed).

### RL-3 — Interim timesheet review authority

*Date:* 21 October 2026 · *Ruled by:* ct · *Governs:* PEG-017, PEG-088

Interim review authority (ct-ruled 21 Oct 2026): only a workspace owner or administrator other than the person who logged the time, with current access to every entry's recorded project, may return or approve a submitted timesheet. No one, and no agent, approves or returns their own time. Time on personal no-project tasks stays owner-only and cannot be submitted, and a later reviewed share does not attach existing entries. Approval sends nothing outward; handoff to a provider is PEG-042. The interim reviewer boundary was ct-ruled on 21 Oct 2026 (owner/admin other than the person who logged the time, with current access to all linked work; no self-approval; personal time stays private and unsubmitted; no outbound action), and delegated review is PEG-088.

### RL-4 — Numeric goal sources

*Date:* 22 October 2026 (approve with change) · *Ruled by:* ct · *Governs:* PEG-014, PEG-027, PEG-062

Numeric-source authority (ct, 22 Oct): only the current active human goal owner authorizes a source, by an explicit audited authorization of that person's own choice (an administrative record, not legal consent), for one goal. An API credential publishes observations to that goal only, grants no data read and no outbound action, and confers no identity or authority on whoever holds it. Every source has a finite owner-chosen expiry (30 days is the engineering bound, not policy). Revocation, expiry or a change of the goal's accountable owner ENDS the source for good: it never resumes, whoever holds the seat afterwards. An ended API source yields no progress, even under an override, shows its named cause, and keeps its observation history visible with each observation's source and authorizer; rows from a reviewed CSV, checked by a person when applied, outlast their source. Named editors still make native check-ins and observations, while agents and guests never publish. Numeric-source authority is ct's ruling of 22 Oct (approve with change): the current active human owner's explicit audited authorization, not legal consent; one goal, publish-only, no read or outbound action; finite owner-chosen expiry (30 days is an engineering bound); revocation, expiry or ownership loss ends the source for good; an ended API source yields no progress and a named cause with its history visible; reviewed CSV rows outlast their source; a credential confers no identity or authority on any holder. PEG-027 and PEG-062 inherit that boundary.
